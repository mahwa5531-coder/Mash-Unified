# Understanding Internal Control

## Purpose
Obtain understanding of internal control components per SA 315. Evaluate design and implementation, identify controls to test, and assess ICFR per Sec 143(3)(i). Basis for risk response and reliance.

## Scope
- USE WHEN: Every statutory audit at planning; controls reliance intended for substantive reduction.
- DO NOT USE WHEN: Substantive-only approach already decided due to weak controls.
- AUDIT AREAS: Control environment, risk assessment process, IS, control activities, monitoring, ITGCs.

## Audit Objective
- Assertions: Control design and implementation understood; ICFR opinion basis obtained.
- Framework: SA 315, SA 330, SA 260; Sec 143(3)(i), Sec 134(5)(e); COSO 2013.

## Workflow Dependencies
- REQUIRES: Understanding Entity, Risk Assessment.
- FEEDS: Audit Sampling, Control Testing, Substantive Procedures, Audit Reporting.

## Required Inputs
- Process flowcharts, narratives, RACI matrices.
- Policy and procedure manuals (finance, procurement, HR, IT).
- Organisational chart, segregation of duties matrix.
- Audit committee charter, internal audit reports.
- IT general controls documentation, system architecture.
- Prior-year control deficiencies and remediation status.

## Optional Inputs
- SOC 1 / SOC 2 reports for service organisations (SA 402).
- Internal financial control (IFC) reports filed by listed Co.
- Risk management committee reports.
- Whistleblower policy and complaints log.

## Knowledge
- SA 315: Understand 5 components — control environment, risk assessment, IS (info & comm), control activities, monitoring.
- Sec 134(5)(e): Directors' responsibility for ICFR; Sec 143(3)(i): Auditor reports on adequacy and operating effectiveness of ICFR (listed Co.).
- SA 330: Test controls only if design + implementation satisfactory AND reliance planned.
- SA 260: Communicate control deficiencies to those charged with governance.
- SA 402: Service organisation controls — obtain SOC report or perform procedures.
- COSO 2013: 17 principles across 5 components.
- ITGCs: Access controls, change management, operations, program development.

## Workflow
1. Understand control environment — integrity, ethical values, board independence, Org structure, HR policies.
   - IF weak control environment → substantive-only, do not rely on controls.
2. Understand entity's risk assessment process — how risks to FS are identified and addressed.
3. Understand information system (IS) — capture, process, report transactions:
   - Major classes of transactions, significant accounts, disclosure processes.
   - IF ERP/system used → evaluate ITGCs and application controls.
4. Understand control activities — authorisation, reconciliations, segregation, physical controls, IT controls.
5. Understand monitoring of controls — internal audit, self-assessments, corrective action.
6. Perform walk-throughs for each significant process:
   - Revenue, procurement, payroll, inventory, FA, treasury, JE.
   - Trace one transaction end-to-end; confirm control points operate as designed.
7. Identify controls to test:
   - IF control mitigates significant risk → test mandatory.
   - IF control operating effectively reduces substantive work → test optional but recommended.
8. Evaluate design effectiveness — does control meet objective?
9. Evaluate implementation — is control in operation? Evidence of execution?
   - IF design or implementation weak → do not rely; substantive only.
10. Assess ICFR for Sec 143(3)(i) reporting (listed Co.) — aggregate deficiencies.
11. Document in ICFR matrix — control ID, description, risk addressed, test planned.
12. Communicate significant deficiencies to audit committee (SA 260).

## Decision Points
- D1: Control environment strong? YES → proceed with reliance plan. NO → substantive only.
- D2: Control design effective? YES → test implementation. NO → do not rely.
- D3: Control implemented? YES → test operating effectiveness (SA 330). NO → do not rely.
- D4: Significant risk mitigated by control? YES → mandatory controls test. NO → substantive response.
- D5: Listed Co.? YES → mandatory ICFR opinion per Sec 143(3)(i). NO → assess for management letter.
- D6: Service organisation used (SaaS, BPO)? YES → obtain SOC report per SA 402. NO → continue.

## Professional Skepticism Probes
- Are controls documented the same as controls actually operating?
- Are walk-through transactions genuinely representative, or pre-selected by management?
- Is segregation of duties real, or overridden by KMP?
- Are internal audit findings acted upon, or ignored?
- Do IT controls address recent cyber threats?
- Are manual controls consistently applied, or only during audit?
- Is management override detected and prevented?

## Considerations
- IT environment: ERP (SAP, Oracle), SaaS, end-user computing (Excel models).
- Group audit: Evaluate component controls; consider SA 600.
- Service organisations: SA 402 — obtain SOC 1 / ISAE 3402 reports.
- Manual vs automated: Automated controls more reliable if ITGCs strong.
- Management override: Always a risk — JE testing mandatory.
- Deficiency aggregation: Multiple minor deficiencies may aggregate to significant.

## Common Risks
- Over-reliance on documented controls without walk-through.
- Skipping ITGCs for ERP-driven processes.
- Missing management override risk.
- Inadequate evaluation of service organisation controls.
- Not testing controls over significant risks mandatorily.
- Treating ICFR as compliance exercise, not risk-driven.

## Fraud Triggers (SA 240)
- Management override documented or suspected — JE testing mandatory.
- KMP bypassing controls — override risk.
- Lack of segregation of duties at top level.
- Internal audit findings ignored by management.
- Manual JE near year-end by senior management.
- Whistleblower complaints on financial misconduct.
- Repeated control deficiencies without remediation.

## Common Pitfalls
- Walk-throughs done but not documented.
- Controls tested but not linked to risks addressed.
- ITGCs skipped assuming ERP is reliable.
- ICFR opinion without adequate testing.
- Deficiencies communicated to management but not audit committee.

## Validation
- [ ] All 5 COSO components understood and documented.
- [ ] Walk-throughs performed for each significant process.
- [ ] Controls linked to risks in ICFR matrix.
- [ ] ITGCs evaluated for ERP-driven processes.
- [ ] Service organisation controls assessed (SA 402).
- [ ] ICFR opinion basis obtained (listed Co.).
- [ ] Significant deficiencies communicated to audit committee (SA 260).

## Expected Outputs
- Internal control understanding memo (5 COSO components).
- Process flowcharts / narratives.
- Walk-through documentation.
- ICFR matrix (control, risk, test).
- Control deficiencies summary for SA 260 communication.
- ICFR opinion working paper (listed Co.).

## Failure Conditions
- Management refuses access to controls documentation.
- Walk-throughs cannot be performed.
- ICFR cannot be assessed for listed entity.

## Escalation Conditions
- Significant deficiency in ICFR → escalate to audit committee, consider Sec 143(3)(i) qualification.
- Management override evidence → STOP, trigger SA 240 workflow.
- IT environment compromised (cyber breach) → STOP, escalate, assess FS impact.
- Service organisation controls inadequate → STOP, perform direct procedures or scope limitation.
