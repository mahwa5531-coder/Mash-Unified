# Analytical Procedures

## Purpose
Perform substantive analytical procedures per SA 520. Develop expectations, compare to recorded amounts, investigate significant differences. Use ratio, trend, reasonableness, predictive analytics.

## Scope
- USE WHEN: Substantive evidence via analytics; risk assessment; final review.
- DO NOT USE WHEN: Weak internal controls + high risk → use tests of details.
- AUDIT AREAS: Revenue, expenses, payroll, interest, depreciation, accruals, ratios.

## Audit Objective
- Assertions: Occurrence, completeness, accuracy, cut-off (limited for cut-off).
- Framework: SA 520, SA 315 (risk assessment), SA 570 (going concern), SA 700 (final review).

## Workflow Dependencies
- REQUIRES: Trial Balance Validation, Materiality, Data Profiling.
- FEEDS: All execution skills, Evaluation of Misstatements, Going Concern, Final Review.

## Required Inputs
- Audited prior-year financials (3-5 years for trend).
- Current-year TB and draft FS.
- Industry benchmarks, peer ratios.
- Non-financial data (production volume, headcount, capacity, units sold).
- Budget vs actual management reports.

## Optional Inputs
- Sector reports, macroeconomic data (inflation, GDP, commodity prices).
- Inflation indices (WPI, CPI).
- RBI repo rate, exchange rates (for finance cost, forex).

## Knowledge
- SA 520.5: Develop expectation; compare to recorded; investigate significant difference.
- SA 520.6: Substantive analytics require predictable relationship and reliable data.
- SA 520.8: Investigate significant differences — corroboration required.
- SA 315: Analytics for risk assessment at planning.
- SA 570: Going concern — trend of losses, negative ratios, liquidity stress.
- SA 700: Final review analytics across full FS.
- Ratio categories: Profitability, liquidity, leverage, turnover, coverage.

## Workflow
1. Identify accounts suitable for analytics:
   - Predictable (interest = rate × principal; depreciation = rate × cost).
   - Volume-driven (revenue, COGS, payroll).
   - Trend-stable (rent, insurance, subscription).
2. Determine type of analytics:
   - Ratio analysis (gross margin, current ratio, debtor days, inventory days).
   - Trend analysis (YoY, monthly, 3-5 year).
   - Reasonableness (re-computation based on drivers).
   - Predictive (regression, time-series).
3. Develop expectation:
   - Based on prior-year actual ± known changes.
   - Based on non-financial drivers (volume × price).
   - Based on industry benchmarks.
4. Set threshold for investigation:
   - Difference > PM or > 5% deviation — whichever lower.
   - Lower threshold for significant risk.
5. Compare recorded to expectation.
6. Investigate significant differences:
   - Inquire management; obtain corroborating evidence.
   - IF explanation plausible + corroborated → accept.
   - IF no explanation OR uncorroborated → perform tests of details.
7. Document expectation, comparison, investigation, conclusion.
8. For final review (SA 700):
   - Apply analytics across full FS.
   - Focus on consistency, plausibility, relationships between items.
9. For going concern (SA 570):
   - Trend analysis on liquidity, leverage, profitability ratios.
   - IF declining trend → escalate, assess management plan.
10. Update analytics if material changes occur during audit.
11. Use analytics for risk assessment (SA 315) at planning — refresh during audit.

## Decision Points
- D1: Relationship predictable + data reliable? YES → substantive analytics acceptable. NO → tests of details.
- D2: Difference > threshold? YES → investigate, corroborate. NO → document.
- D3: Explanation corroborated? YES → accept. NO → tests of details or qualify.
- D4: Significant risk area? YES → analytics insufficient alone, combine with tests of details. NO → analytics may suffice.
- D5: Going concern indicators? YES → SA 570 workflow. NO → standard.

## Professional Skepticism Probes
- Is the expectation truly independent, or based on management's narrative?
- Are deviations explained by genuine business reasons, or evaded?
- Are corroborating sources reliable, or management-prepared?
- Do ratios align with industry, or deviate unexplained?
- Is the trend stable, or volatile with weak rationale?
- Have non-financial drivers been verified independently?

## Considerations
- Data reliability: Source system, ITGCs, completeness.
- Comparability: Accounting policy changes per Ind AS 8.
- Inflation: Adjust for inflation in long-term trends.
- Seasonality: Adjust for seasonal patterns.
- Materiality: Difference threshold = PM or lower for significant risk.
- Group audits: Apply analytics at component and consolidated level.

## Common Risks
- Using management's expectation without independent development.
- Accepting explanations without corroboration.
- Not investigating significant differences.
- Over-reliance on analytics for significant risk areas.
- Not adjusting for accounting policy changes.
- Skipping final review analytics (SA 700).

## Fraud Triggers (SA 240)
- Significant unexplained differences.
- Management explanations inconsistent with non-financial data.
- Trend reversals near year-end without business rationale.
- Ratios deviating significantly from industry without explanation.
- Volatile trends in stable industry.
- Corroborating evidence unavailable or management-prepared.

## Common Pitfalls
- Analytics done at planning but not refreshed at completion.
- Expectation not documented.
- Investigation not documented.
- Corroboration skipped — accepting management explanation.
- Over-reliance on analytics for significant risk.
- Not adjusting for policy changes or inflation.

## Validation
- [ ] Accounts suitable for analytics identified.
- [ ] Expectation developed independently.
- [ ] Threshold for investigation set (PM or lower).
- [ ] Significant differences investigated and corroborated.
- [ ] Documentation complete — expectation, comparison, investigation, conclusion.
- [ ] Final review analytics (SA 700) performed.
- [ ] Going concern analytics (SA 570) considered.

## Expected Outputs
- Analytical procedures working paper (expectation, comparison, investigation).
- Ratio analysis with industry benchmark.
- Trend analysis (YoY, monthly, multi-year).
- Reasonableness re-computation.
- Investigation log with corroborating evidence.
- Conclusion on sufficiency of analytics vs need for tests of details.

## Failure Conditions
- Data unreliable — analytics cannot be performed.
- Expectation cannot be developed independently.
- Significant differences not investigated.

## Escalation Conditions
- Significant unexplained difference → STOP, perform tests of details.
- Management explanation inconsistent with evidence → STOP, escalate to partner.
- Going concern indicators → STOP, trigger SA 570 workflow.
- Fraud indicators in trend/ratio → STOP, trigger SA 240 workflow.
