# Risk Assessment

## Purpose
Identify, assess, and respond to risks of material misstatement at FS and assertion levels per SA 315 and SA 330. Document significant risks, fraud risks, and design risk responses.

## Scope
- USE WHEN: Every statutory audit, after entity understanding; refreshed if conditions change.
- DO NOT USE WHEN: Performance of specific substantive procedures (use cycle-specific skills).
- AUDIT AREAS: FS-level risks, assertion-level risks, significant risks, fraud risks.

## Audit Objective
- Assertions: All material risks identified, assessed, and responded to.
- Framework: SA 315, SA 330, SA 240, SA 550, SA 570, SA 580.

## Workflow Dependencies
- REQUIRES: Understanding Entity, Materiality Determination.
- FEEDS: Audit Planning, all execution skills, Internal Control Understanding, Fraud Risk Assessment.

## Required Inputs
- Entity profile memo, related-party register.
- Trial balance, draft financials, prior-year comparatives.
- Risk register from management, internal audit reports.
- Industry risk reports, regulatory updates.
- Prior-year audit issues, management letter.

## Optional Inputs
- Peer benchmarking, analyst reports.
- Group audit instructions (component risk profile).
- Whistleblower complaints, fraud allegations.

## Knowledge
- SA 315: Assess risk at FS level and assertion level; identify significant risks.
- SA 330: Design and perform responses to assessed risks.
- SA 240: Presumed fraud risk for revenue recognition and management override of controls.
- SA 550: Related parties — significant risk by default.
- SA 570: Going concern — assess indicators and management plan.
- SA 580: Written representations from management.
- Sec 143(12): Report fraud ≥ ₹1 crore via Form ADT-4.

## Workflow
1. Perform risk assessment procedures — inquiry, observation, inspection, analytical review.
2. Identify FS-level risks (broad, pervasive):
   - Management override of controls (presumed — SA 240).
   - Going concern.
   - Weak IT environment.
   - Weak governance / control environment.
3. Identify assertion-level risks by cycle:
   - Revenue (presumed fraud risk): occurrence, cut-off.
   - Receivables: existence, valuation (ECL).
   - Inventory: existence, valuation (NRV).
   - Fixed assets: existence, impairment.
   - Investments: valuation, classification.
   - Borrowings: completeness, classification.
   - Provisions/estimates: completeness, measurement (SA 540).
   - Related parties: completeness, disclosure (SA 550).
   - Tax: GST, IT, deferred tax completeness.
4. Identify significant risks (require special audit response):
   - Significant risk = high likelihood + material amount + pervasive.
   - Always include: revenue recognition, management override, RPT, fraud risk.
5. Assess risk of fraud per SA 240:
   - Brainstorming session mandatory — engagement team.
   - Document fraud risks, schemes, scenarios.
6. Assess inherent and control risks separately.
   - IF controls strong → rely, reduce substantive.
   - IF controls weak → substantive only, larger sample.
7. Design risk responses per SA 330:
   - Significant risk → mandatory substantive + controls + journal entry testing.
   - Routine risk → substantive analytics or sampling.
   - Low risk → analytics + walk-through.
8. Map risk to specific audit procedures and team member.
9. Determine nature, timing, extent — interim vs year-end, sample sizes.
10. Document risk assessment summary in audit file; partner sign-off.
11. Re-assess if new information emerges (fraud, M&A, going concern issues).

## Decision Points
- D1: Significant risk identified? YES → mandatory substantive + controls testing. NO → standard procedures.
- D2: Revenue fraud risk (presumed)? YES → mandatory cut-off, JE testing, larger sample. NO → document rationale.
- D3: Management override risk? YES → JE testing, accounting estimate review, significant transactions review. NO → document.
- D4: Controls effective? YES → test controls, rely for substantive reduction. NO → substantive only.
- D5: Going concern indicators? YES → SA 570 workflow. NO → standard.
- D6: Related parties significant? YES → SA 550 detailed testing. NO → standard disclosure testing.

## Professional Skepticism Probes
- Have we brainstormed how fraud could occur — not just whether it has?
- Is revenue pressure driving aggressive recognition?
- Are estimates biased toward earnings targets?
- Are related-party transactions genuinely arm's length?
- Has management's explanation for unusual trends been independently tested?
- Are we over-relying on prior-year risk assessment?
- Have whistleblowers/analysts raised concerns we dismissed?

## Considerations
- Risk assessment is iterative — update throughout audit.
- Group audit: Consider component risks; evaluate component auditor work.
- IT environment: Identify ITGC risks, application controls, end-user computing.
- Estimates: SA 540 — bias, assumptions, management's point estimate vs range.
- Related parties: Sec 188 approval, Ind AS 24 disclosure, completeness of identification.
- First-year: Include opening balances risk (SA 510).

## Common Risks
- Not identifying revenue as significant risk (presumed under SA 240).
- Missing related-party transactions in identification phase.
- Over-reliance on prior-year risk profile without refresh.
- Skipping brainstorming session for fraud.
- Disconnect between risks and audit procedures.
- Treating risk assessment as one-time, not iterative.

## Fraud Triggers (SA 240)
- Revenue growth inconsistent with industry/peers.
- Significant RPT not at arm's length.
- Frequent KMP changes, weak governance.
- Aggressive accounting estimates.
- Recurring losses with continued revenue growth.
- Promoter pledges, financial pressure.
- Unexplained year-end transactions.

## Common Pitfalls
- Risk register copied from prior year without update.
- No documentation of brainstorming session.
- Procedures not linked to specific risks.
- Significant risks identified but no special response designed.
- Skipping JE testing on management override risk.

## Validation
- [ ] Risk assessment procedures performed and documented.
- [ ] FS-level and assertion-level risks identified.
- [ ] Significant risks identified and rationale documented.
- [ ] SA 240 brainstorming session documented.
- [ ] Risk responses mapped to each significant risk.
- [ ] Partner sign-off on risk assessment summary.
- [ ] Risk reassessed if material changes occur.

## Expected Outputs
- Risk assessment summary memo (signed by partner).
- Fraud brainstorming memo (SA 240).
- Risk-response matrix (risk → procedure → team → timing).
- Significant risks register.
- Re-assessment log (if applicable).

## Failure Conditions
- No documented brainstorming session for fraud.
- Risk register not linked to audit procedures.
- Significant risks identified without special response.
- Partner has not approved risk assessment.

## Escalation Conditions
- New significant risk identified mid-audit → STOP, update strategy, partner approval.
- Fraud risk with management involvement → STOP, trigger SA 240 workflow, consider Sec 143(12).
- Going concern material uncertainty → STOP, trigger SA 570.
- Whistleblower allegation of fraud → STOP, escalate to partner, investigate.
