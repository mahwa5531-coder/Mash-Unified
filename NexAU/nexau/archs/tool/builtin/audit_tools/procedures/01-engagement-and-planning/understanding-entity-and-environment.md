# Understanding Entity and Environment

## Purpose
Obtain understanding of entity, its environment, applicable framework, and internal control per SA 315. Foundation for risk assessment and audit strategy.

## Scope
- USE WHEN: Every statutory audit at planning stage; refresh for continuing clients.
- DO NOT USE WHEN: Performance of substantive procedures (use specific cycle skills).
- AUDIT AREAS: Industry, regulatory, ownership, governance, business strategy, financial performance.

## Audit Objective
- Assertions: Entity profile understood; risks of material misstatement identified.
- Framework: SA 315, SA 250, Ind AS 1 (going concern disclosures), Sec 134/134(5) Companies Act.

## Workflow Dependencies
- REQUIRES: Engagement Acceptance.
- FEEDS: Risk Assessment, Internal Control Understanding, Audit Planning, Going Concern.

## Required Inputs
- Audited prior-year financials and annual report.
- Industry reports, competitor analysis, sector outlook.
- Organisational chart, shareholding pattern, board composition.
- KMP list, related-party register (Sec 188).
- Business plan, strategic plan, budgets.
- Regulatory licenses and registrations (GST, RBI, SEBI, IRDAI, EPFO, ESIC).
- Press releases, analyst reports (listed Co.).

## Optional Inputs
- Internal audit reports, secretarial audit report.
- Group audit instructions (if component).
- Site visits / facility tours.
- Customer/supplier concentration analysis.

## Knowledge
- SA 315: Understand entity, environment, and internal control to identify risks.
- Sec 134(5): Directors' responsibility for internal financial controls (ICFR).
- Sec 177/178: Audit committee and nomination/remuneration committee roles.
- Ind AS 1.25-26: Going concern — material uncertainties disclosure.
- CARO 2020: Clauses on loans, RPT, default, fraud — context for understanding.
- RBI/SEBI/IRDAI directions for regulated entities.
- Sec 188 / Ind AS 24: Related-party disclosures.

## Workflow
1. Obtain industry understanding — size, growth, competition, regulation, technology disruption.
   - IF regulated entity (NBFC, bank, insurance) → identify sector-specific risks.
2. Understand ownership and governance:
   - Promoter holding, pledge status, FII/DII stake (listed Co.).
   - Board composition, independent directors, audit committee composition per Sec 177.
   - IF promoter pledge > 50% → going concern and related-party risk.
3. Understand business strategy and objectives:
   - Product mix, customer concentration, geographic spread.
   - IF new product/geography → integration/execution risk.
4. Understand financial performance:
   - YoY revenue, margin, working capital, debt service.
   - IF margins deviate significantly from peers → benchmark for fraud risk.
5. Understand applicable framework:
   - Ind AS for listed/large Co.; AS for others; sector-specific (NBFC directions, IRDAI).
   - IF first-time Ind AS adoption → use SA 710 (comparatives).
6. Identify measurement and disclosure issues:
   - Fair value, impairment, deferred tax, lease accounting, revenue recognition.
7. Understand objectives and strategies → relate to business risks → FS risks.
8. Review prior-year audit issues, management letter, internal audit reports.
   - IF recurring issues → control deficiency; raise risk.
9. Assess going concern indicators:
   - Negative net worth, recurring losses, debt covenant breach, liquidity stress.
10. Document understanding in entity profile memo; identify preliminary risks for SA 315.
11. Update understanding if material changes occur during audit.

## Decision Points
- D1: Industry regulated (NBFC/bank/insurance)? YES → sector-specific risks identified. NO → general risks.
- D2: Promoter pledged shares > 50%? YES → going concern + RPT risk. NO → standard.
- D3: Going concern indicators present? YES → SA 570 workflow triggered. NO → standard.
- D4: First-time Ind AS adoption? YES → SA 710 + comparatives testing. NO → standard.
- D5: Major business change (M&A, new segment)? YES → update risk profile. NO → continue.

## Professional Skepticism Probes
- Are margins plausible vs peers — or manipulated?
- Are KMP changes explained or a red flag?
- Is the business model genuinely understood, or summarised from annual report?
- Are related-party structures transparent or deliberately complex?
- Does governance appear functional, or rubber-stamp?
- Are prior-year issues genuinely resolved or recurring?
- Is going concern support robust or based on optimistic assumptions?

## Considerations
- Group structure: Inter-company transactions, transfer pricing, consolidation.
- IT environment: ERP complexity, third-party platforms, cyber risk.
- Geography: Multi-state GST, multi-country FEMA, exchange risk.
- Seasonality: Affects revenue cut-off, inventory, working capital.
- Related parties: Sec 188 approval, Ind AS 24 disclosure, arm's length pricing.
- ESG and sustainability: Emerging disclosure requirements.

## Common Risks
- Reading annual report without independent verification.
- Missing related-party relationships (e.g., common directorships).
- Overlooking regulatory non-compliance history.
- Not connecting business risks to FS risks.
- Ignoring KMP/management changes as fraud indicator.
- Inadequate understanding of complex group structure.

## Fraud Triggers (SA 240)
- Complex group structure with no clear business rationale.
- Frequent changes in KMP, board, CFO without explanation.
- Related-party transactions at non-arm's length.
- Promoter share pledges — pressure for performance.
- Regulatory actions, search/seizure, pending investigations.
- Negative net worth with continued aggressive accounting.
- Customer/supplier concentration with related parties.
- Significant off-balance-sheet exposures.

## Common Pitfalls
- Treating prior-year understanding as automatically current.
- Not documenting specific industry knowledge.
- Skipping going concern assessment at planning.
- Missing CARO 2020 reporting implications.
- Not identifying related-party structures early enough.

## Validation
- [ ] Industry and competitive landscape documented.
- [ ] Ownership and governance structure understood.
- [ ] Business strategy and objectives identified.
- [ ] Applicable framework confirmed.
- [ ] Related-party register reviewed.
- [ ] Preliminary risks identified for SA 315.
- [ ] Going concern indicators assessed.

## Expected Outputs
- Entity profile memo (industry, governance, strategy, financials).
- Preliminary risks register for SA 315.
- Related-party register (initial).
- Going concern initial assessment.
- Documentation of framework applicability.

## Failure Conditions
- Management refuses to provide governance/ownership documents.
- Inability to obtain industry/regulatory information.
- Related-party register not provided.

## Escalation Conditions
- Significant undisclosed related parties → STOP, escalate to partner.
- Going concern uncertainty with no management plan → STOP, trigger SA 570.
- Regulatory non-compliance with financial impact → STOP, escalate, consider qualification.
- Complex group structure masking fraud → STOP, escalate to Risk Management Partner.
