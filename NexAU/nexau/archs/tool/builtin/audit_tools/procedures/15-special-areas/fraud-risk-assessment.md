# Fraud Risk Assessment

## Purpose
Identify, assess, and respond to fraud risks; report material fraud per SA 240 and Sec 143(12).

## Scope
- USE WHEN: Every statutory audit — mandatory fraud risk discussion and response.
- DO NOT USE WHEN: Never skip — fraud risk assessment is mandatory in all audits.
- AUDIT AREAS: Revenue recognition, management override, asset misappropriation, financial statement fraud, related party fraud.

## Audit Objective
- Assertions: All assertions impacted by fraud risk.
- Framework: SA 240; SA 315; Sec 143(12); Rule 13 of Companies (Audit and Auditors) Rules 2014.

## Workflow Dependencies
- REQUIRES: Risk Assessment, Journal Entry Testing, Revenue Testing, Related Party Identification, Going Concern Assessment.
- FEEDS: Audit Conclusion, Audit Reporting, Sec 143(12) Reporting.

## Required Inputs
- Management representations on fraud; prior-year fraud findings; whistle-blower complaints.
- Board minutes; audit committee minutes; internal audit reports; risk management reports.
- JE listing; revenue disaggregation; related party list; significant unusual transactions.
- KMP changes; shareholding pattern; promoter pledges.

## Optional Inputs
- Forensic reports; SEBI / regulator correspondence; media reports; competitor analysis.

## Knowledge
- SA 240.13: Discussion among engagement team — susceptibility to fraud; exchange of ideas; brainstorm.
- SA 240.26-27: Presume fraud risk in revenue recognition; presume risk of management override of controls.
- SA 240.32: Test appropriateness of JE; identify unusual entries; test timing (year-end, weekend, holiday).
- SA 240.41: If fraud identified — communicate to management / TCWG; assess implications; determine effect on audit.
- Sec 143(12): Report fraud ≥₹1 cr to Central Government within 30 days (Form ADT-4); <₹1 cr to audit committee.

## Workflow
1. Hold engagement team brainstorming session (SA 240.13) — discuss where and how FS may be susceptible to fraud; identify locations, accounts, disclosures.
2. Identify fraud risk factors — Pressure: declining margins, missed forecasts, debt covenants, promoter pledges, KMP incentives tied to earnings; Opportunity: weak controls, dominant management, complex structure, related parties, IT access; Rationalisation: aggressive accounting history, prior adjustments, regulatory inquiries.
3. Presume fraud risk in revenue recognition (SA 240.26) — identify risk areas (cut-off, returns, consignment, bill-and-hold, related party sales, round-tripping).
4. Presume management override risk (SA 240.27) — test JE appropriateness; review estimates bias; assess significant unusual transactions.
5. Perform JE testing (link to Journal Entry Testing skill) — identify JEs by amount, timing (year-end, weekend, holiday), user (KMP), source (manual, top-side).
6. Test significant unusual transactions — identify transactions outside normal course; assess substance; verify business rationale.
7. Test related party transactions (link to Related Party Transactions Testing) — verify undisclosed RPT; assess substance vs form; check round-tripping.
8. Test revenue cut-off and recognition — verify cut-off; test returns; assess consignment / bill-and-hold; review sales to related parties.
9. Test asset misappropriation risks — inventory shrinkage; receivables collections; vendor master manipulation; payroll ghost employees.
10. Evaluate fraud evidence — IF fraud identified THEN communicate to management / TCWG; assess materiality; determine scope impact; consider Sec 143(12) reporting.
11. Document fraud risk assessment, response, and conclusion.
12. Communicate with TCWG per SA 260; assess need for Sec 143(12) reporting (≥₹1 cr). Update audit opinion and reporting; consider withdrawal (Sec 144) if management integrity compromised.

## Decision Points
- D1: Fraud risk in revenue recognition identified? YES → presume risk (SA 240.26); design specific response. NO → challenge — presumption mandatory.
- D2: Management override risk identified? YES → JE testing + estimates + unusual transactions. NO → presumption mandatory — challenge.
- D3: JE testing reveals unusual entries? YES → investigate; assess fraud indicator. NO → document conclusion.
- D4: Significant unusual transaction lacks business rationale? YES → fraud risk; extended testing. NO → document.
- D5: Fraud identified material? YES → communicate to TCWG; assess Sec 143(12) ≥₹1 cr reporting. NO → document.

## Professional Skepticism Probes
- Is revenue growth aligned with industry, or anomalous?
- Are JEs by KMP at year-end genuinely business-driven?
- Are significant unusual transactions with related parties or offshore entities?
- Are estimates consistently biased toward management objectives?
- Is KMP turnover / auditor change indicative of underlying concerns?

## Considerations
- Fraud triangle (pressure, opportunity, rationalisation) — all three required.
- Revenue recognition presumption cannot be rebutted; specific response mandatory.
- JE testing: focus on top-side entries, manual entries, year-end, holiday entries, KMP-initiated.
- Estimates bias: compare prior-year estimates to actuals; assess directional consistency.
- Sec 143(12) threshold: ₹1 cr (single or aggregate); report within 30 days via Form ADT-4.

## Common Risks
- Revenue recognition via cut-off manipulation / round-tripping.
- Management override via top-side JEs at year-end.
- Estimates biased to meet targets (impairment, ECL, warranty).
- Related party transactions at non-arm's length.
- Asset misappropriation via inventory / receivables / payroll.

## Fraud Triggers (SA 240)
- Revenue spikes at year-end with no economic substance.
- Round-tripping through shell entities / related parties.
- Top-side JEs by CFO / MD at year-end reversing in next period.
- Estimates consistently reversed next year (cookie jar).
- Customer bankruptcy post year-end on year-end receivable (ECL understated).

## Common Pitfalls
- Treating fraud risk assessment as a formality checklist.
- Not brainstorming as a team; documenting generic risks.
- Not presuming revenue recognition and management override risks.
- JE testing without focus on KMP / top-side / year-end.
- Not testing significant unusual transactions.

## Validation
- [ ] Engagement team brainstorming session held; risks documented.
- [ ] Fraud risk factors (triangle) identified.
- [ ] Revenue recognition and management override risk presumed; responses designed.
- [ ] JE testing performed with focus on KMP / top-side / year-end.
- [ ] Significant unusual transactions tested.

## Expected Outputs
- Fraud risk assessment master working paper (brainstorming memo).
- Presumed fraud risks response memo (revenue, override).
- JE testing results memo (unusual entries investigated).
- Significant unusual transactions testing memo.

## Failure Conditions
- Management refuses to provide JE listing or restricts access.
- Audit committee / TCWG unwilling to discuss fraud risks.
- Forensic / regulator correspondence not produced.

## Escalation Conditions
- Material fraud identified (≥₹1 cr) → STOP, Sec 143(12) reporting, Form ADT-4, partner consultation.
- Management integrity compromised (involvement in fraud, suppression) → STOP, consider resignation (Sec 144), partner consultation.
- Fraud pervasive affecting FS as a whole → STOP, disclaimer of opinion (SA 705), partner consultation.
- Regulator inquiry (SEBI / SFIO / ED) material → STOP, coordinate response, partner consultation.
